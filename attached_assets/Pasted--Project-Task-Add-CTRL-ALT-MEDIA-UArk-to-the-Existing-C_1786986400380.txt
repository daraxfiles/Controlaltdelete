# 4. CREATE "UARK MISSIONS"

## UArk Missions

Create the following starter missions.

---

## Mission 1 — AI Said What About Arkansas?

**Level:** Beginner/Intermediate
**Topics:** AI Literacy, Verification, Local Knowledge

Description:

Students ask an AI system questions about Arkansas, the University of Arkansas, Northwest Arkansas, or a local community.

Students then:

1. Record the AI response.
2. Extract factual claims.
3. Verify claims using credible sources.
4. Identify inaccuracies.
5. Identify missing context.
6. Identify stereotypes or oversimplifications.
7. Produce a corrected evidence-backed media artifact.

Possible outputs:

* infographic
* article
* short video
* podcast
* interactive webpage
* AI fact-check card

Final question:

**What did the AI get right, wrong, or leave out?**

---

## Mission 2 — Who Gets Quoted at UArk?

**Level:** Intermediate
**Topics:** Representation, Journalism, Data Literacy

Students analyze a sample of campus-related media stories.

They identify:

* who is quoted
* student voices
* faculty voices
* administrator voices
* community voices
* whose perspectives dominate
* whose voices may be missing

Students should create a small data visualization and interpretation.

Final question:

**Who gets to speak when our campus stories are told?**

---

## Mission 3 — Razorback Rumor Trace

**Level:** Beginner
**Topics:** Misinformation, Verification

Students investigate a non-sensitive rumor, claim, or piece of questionable information circulating within the university community.

Workflow:

Origin
→ Reposts
→ Changes in wording
→ Evidence used
→ Official/credible sources
→ Verified conclusion

Students should focus on **how information traveled**, not merely whether something was true or false.

Final question:

**How did this claim move through the campus information ecosystem?**

---

## Mission 4 — Algorithm Audit: Razorback Edition

**Level:** Intermediate/Advanced
**Topics:** Algorithmic Literacy, AI Literacy

Teams investigate how different platforms present information about the same topic.

Possible platforms:

* Google
* YouTube
* TikTok
* Instagram
* Reddit
* AI assistants

Students compare:

* what appears first
* what information is prioritized
* what is missing
* commercial influence
* personalization
* recommendations
* potential bias

Final question:

**How might different information systems create different versions of the same reality?**

---

## Mission 5 — UArk Information Desert Map

**Level:** Advanced
**Topics:** Information Access, Design, Community Research

Students investigate where members of the university community actually obtain important information.

Possible populations:

* first-year students
* international students
* graduate students
* first-generation students
* commuter students
* online students
* student parents

Students map sources such as:

* university websites
* Blackboard/LMS
* email
* advisors
* faculty
* GroupMe
* Reddit
* TikTok
* Instagram
* friends
* student organizations

Students identify gaps where important information is difficult to locate or understand.

Final product should propose or create an information resource addressing the gap.

---

## Mission 6 — Public Document Decoder

**Level:** Beginner/Intermediate
**Topics:** Information Design, Accessibility

Students select an appropriate publicly available university or community document and convert complicated information into an accessible public-facing explanation.

Possible outputs:

* infographic
* FAQ
* short video
* visual explainer
* interactive webpage
* podcast
* plain-language guide

Final question:

## Mission 7 — Search Yourself: Razorback Edition

# 7. LOCALIZE THE REBOOT PROTOCOL

Keep the existing seven-stage Reboot Protocol.

For UArk investigations, add relevant prompts.

## Stage 1 — Crash Report

Ask:

* What information problem are you investigating?
* Who in the university/community is affected?
* Why does it matter?
* What do people currently believe?

## Stage 2 — System Trace

Ask:

* Where did this information originate?
* What official sources exist?
* What unofficial sources exist?
* How does the information circulate?
* Who controls or produces the information?
* Whose voices are missing?

## Stage 3 — Red Team Room

Ask:

* What evidence could challenge your current conclusion?
* Are you making assumptions?
* What alternative explanation exists?
* What evidence would change your mind?
* Have you represented people fairly?

## Stage 4 — Build the Patch

Students choose an output:

* article
* podcast
* short documentary
* infographic
* data story
* digital zine
* interactive webpage
* public guide
* AI audit
* accessibility redesign

## Stage 5 — Ship With Receipts

Require an Evidence Receipt.

For each major claim capture:

* claim
* source
* source type
* link/citation
* verification method
* confidence level
* notes
* uncertainty

## Stage 6 — Power Ping

Students identify someone positioned to respond.

Examples of target roles:

* university office
* administrator
* faculty member
* student organization
* journalist
* public official
* community organization

Require respectful, evidence-based communication.

Do not frame Power Pings as harassment, public shaming, or mass campaigning.

## Stage 7 — Patch Notes

Publish the completed investigation as a UArk Media Patch.
